Foil Board Project

This was a project I did at Iolani School in my senior year for Design and Fabrication. For the project I made a custom vacuum bagged carbon hydrofoil board with an EPS foam core. As a senior in the class I was given free use of the shop to make a project of my pleasing. I chose to make a hydrofoil board, otherwise known as foilboard, that fit my specific needs as a rider. The final product came out very well and has served as my main board for the last 2 years.

Building Process

Design

The design of the board was done with a CAD software built for surfboard design called shape3d. I based the design off a popular production prone board that one of my favorite riders has. From that design I included the stepped in rails and far forward wide point. I added much more rocker to the bottom and a little to the nose. This was done because other boards I have ridden were too flat and felt like I was nose diving the whole time. I made the rail shape nice and round at the front and sharp at the tail so that the board would easily release from the water.

Cutting and Preparation

To convert the board from a 3D model to a real world piece of foam I used CNC machining. While we had a CNC in the lab it was not set up to cut surfboards so we hired a local shaper with a surfboard specific CNC. The board was cut from an EPS foam surfboard blank on the CNC. After getting the cut board I had to do some hand sanding to smooth out the ridges caused by the cutting wheel and round out the rails some more. Then we routed holes for the mast tracks and epoxied them into the blank.

Vacuum Bagging

For lamination of the board to increase its strength we uses a wet layup vacuum bagging method with carbon fiber. Then we cut the carbon sheets for the board and used multiple layers. For the bottom of the board we used three increasingly long strips of carbon that were the width of the mast tracks along the length of the board and then a full sheet that wrapped around the rails. These strips exist to provide support at the mast tracks because this is the point of highest load on a foilboard. Then after vacuuming the bottom I prepared the board for the top lamination. For the top we use one long strip that ran along the double stringer of the blank so that they would be reinforced. Then I cut a sheet of carbon for the footwell area to prevent pressure dings forming and finally a layer that wrapped around the rails of the board. The board was vacuum bagged again to achieve full strength.

Wet Layup Lamination

After vacuum bagging was complete any blemishes and error were sanded out and the whole board was given a rough sanding to prepare it for a wet layup of fiberglass for a little bit more strength and to protect the fragile carbon from dings. Each side was laid up with 4 oz fiberglass bonded with surfboard epoxy. After both sides were complete the board was once again rough sanded so that it was ready for the final hotcoat.

Hot Coat and Finishing

The last non sanding step was applying a hot coat to both sides of the board to remove the screen door pattern left by the fiberglass. For this step I used a white epoxy resin dye to give the board its final white color. After the hot coat was applied to the top and bottom of the board, I sanded the whole thing to 220 grit. Then I sanded down the lamination over the mast tracks to reveal them. There were some small airholes around the mast tracks so I injected some epoxy into them to seal them off.

Lessons Learned

While I had done lots of fiberglass work in the past, this was my first time working with vacuum bagging and carbon fiber. It taught me a lot about the process of composite manufacturing, especially at higher levels where vacuum bagging is a necessity. I learned how hard it is to work with composite materials and the dangers that their manufacturing comes with. If I were to do this project again, I would want to include strap inserts so I could progress my foiling into a new discipline, and I would sand the board to a finer finish so that it looks prettier. I could also work on my vacuum bagging as the carbon came out looking rough but, I can't complain too much for my first ever time doing it.
